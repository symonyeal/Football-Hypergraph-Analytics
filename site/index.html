A team's attack has a shape that lasts all season, and the few players behind most of its chances can be picked out from the first half

Every chance is treated as the group of the last three players involved before the shot. From a half-season of chances we count how few players took part in all of them, how many chances shared nobody, and how much danger any set of players touches. Every test was written down before the data was opened.

Concentration carries over: a team's numbers in the first half of the season predict its second half

One dot per team, 2015/16 Premier League, La Liga, Serie A and Ligue 1 (80 teams). First half = match weeks 1–19, second half = 20–38. Correlation is Spearman's, with a 95% bootstrap interval over teams. The grey line is "no change".

Mark a few players and see how much of a team's danger runs through them

Coverage = share of the team's open-play expected goals (xG) from chances in which at least one marked player was among the last three involved. Choosing on the first half and checking on the second is the registered test: across teams, the transversal set covers 94% of later danger against 89% for the players with most touches. Coverage shows involvement, not what would happen if a player were marked: teams adapt.

Teams with fewer separate routes did not clearly lose more when their key player was missing

x-axis: separate routes in the first half (most chances sharing no player). y-axis: drop in second-half xG, relative to each opponent's usual concession, in matches the key player missed against matches the player started. Teams with at least 3 matches of each. Observational: rotation and injuries are not random. The "key player" is often one of several tied players (see docs/POSTHOC.md, P1).

Where the ball is decides most box entries; a hole in the block adds a little, and topology adds nothing

Model fitted on 40,000 opponent-half passes from Euro 2020 and the 2022 World Cup (360 frames), tested on Euro 2024. Drag the defenders (orange) and the ball (black); attack goes to the right. The probability is the fitted logistic model's chance that the attacking team has an event in the box within 10 seconds. Orange lines join neighbouring defenders (the thick one is the widest gap); the blue circle is the largest empty circle inside the block.
