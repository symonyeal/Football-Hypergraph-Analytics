Which players does a team's attack run through, and how many separate routes to goal does it have?

Every chance in two public data sets (men's and women's leagues, World Cups, Euros and other tournaments, 2003 to 2025) is reduced to the last three players involved before the shot. Filter by source, competition and season, then pick teams and players to see how much of the danger runs through whom, and how that changes from season to season. Danger is measured in expected goals (xG): the chance a shot of that quality is scored.

Choose what to look at

These filters apply to the team, player and defence views. The open data covers every match of the 2015/16 Premier League, La Liga, Serie A and Ligue 1, four Women's Super League seasons and other women's leagues, World Cups, Euros, Copa América 2024 and AFCON 2023, and Barcelona's La Liga matches from 2004/05 to 2020/21 (StatsBomb open data), plus every match of the 2017/18 Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A and Ligue 1, the 2018 World Cup and Euro 2016 (Wyscout public data, Pappalardo et al. 2019, CC BY 4.0). A team is measured in a season or tournament when it played at least 5 matches with 15 chances. Team histories join seasons by team name, and the two providers sometimes name a club differently.

      Change in the team's xG difference per 90 minutes with the player on the pitch, others held fixed. Rated only with at least 900 minutes on and 270 team minutes off in a half. These ratings repeat between halves but largely reflect team strength: strong teams' regulars all rate high.

      Drag the defenders: where the ball is decides most box entries, and gaps in the block add a little

      Fitted on 40,000 opponent-half passes from Euro 2020 and the 2022 World Cup, tested on Euro 2024. Drag the defenders (orange) and the ball (black); the attack goes to the right. The thick orange line is the widest gap between neighbouring defenders; the blue circle is the largest empty circle inside the block.
